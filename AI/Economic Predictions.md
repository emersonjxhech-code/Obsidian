# Deep Dive: Trade Balance Models for US-China Goods vs. Services — Projections for 2026-2027

## Executive Summary

The US-China trade relationship is undergoing a structural transformation characterized by divergent trajectories between goods and services trade. While the goods trade deficit is narrowing significantly (down 31.6% in 2025 to $202.1 billion), the services trade surplus is expanding (up 16.7% in 2024 to $33.2 billion). Trade flows are rerouting through ASEAN supply chains, obscuring headline numbers while Chinese inputs continue flowing through intermediary countries. Despite tariff-driven distortions, structural imbalances remain largely unchanged due to persistent US fiscal deficits and Chinese household saving patterns.

## 2. Trade Balance Models: Structural Frameworks

## A. Balance of Payments Model

**Fundamental Equation:**

text

`Trade Balance = Exports - Imports = (S - I) + (T - G)`

Where:

- S = Private savings
    
- I = Private investment
    
- T = Government tax revenue
    
- G = Government spending
    

**US Side (Deficit Country):**

- High fiscal deficits (T - G < 0)
    
- Healthy private sector demand (S - I < 0)
    
- Result: Persistent trade and current account deficits
    

**China Side (Surplus Country):**

- High household savings (S elevated)
    
- Subdued domestic consumption (I constrained)
    
- Result: Persistent trade and current account surpluses
    

## B. Structural Imbalance Persistence Model

|Factor|US|China|Impact on Trade Balance|
|---|---|---|---|
|**Fiscal Policy**|Large deficits|Moderate deficits|US imports > exports|
|**Household Savings**|Low (6-7%)|High (30-35%)|China exports > imports|
|**Private Demand**|+3.6% q/q (Q3 2025)|Moderate|Sustains US import demand|
|**Consumption**|Robust|Subdued|China surplus persists|

**Critical Finding:** Unless structural imbalances change through fiscal restraint (US) or consumption boost (China), global configuration remains unchanged: one giant deficit country (US), one giant surplus country (China).

## C. Trade Rerouting/Transshipment Model

**ASEAN Intermediary Effect:**

|HS Code Category|US Deficit with China (Δ)|US Deficit with ASEAN (Δ)|Net Effect|
|---|---|---|---|
|**HS 84 (Computers)**|-$29B|+$43B|+$14B rerouted|
|**HS 85 (Electronics)**|-$70B|+$80B|+$10B rerouted|
|**Laptops/Tablets**|-$24B|+$21B|Nearly complete shift|

**Mechanism:** Chinese inputs flow through ASEAN supply chains, with final assembly in Vietnam, Thailand, Malaysia before export to US.

**China-ASEAN Balance:** China's surplus with ASEAN in HS 85 increased by nearly $70 billion, confirming rerouting pattern.

**High-Tech Goods (HS 84 & 85):**

- US deficit with China in computers/electronics: -$70B decline
    
- US deficit with ASEAN in same category: +$80B increase
    
- Net: Near-complete rerouting through Southeast Asia
    

**Agricultural Products:**

- China committed to purchasing US soybeans, sorghum, logs (Oct 2025)
    
- China suspended tariffs on US agricultural products until Dec 31, 2026
    

**AI Infrastructure & Consumer Electronics:**

- Now driving US imports from ASEAN
    
- Reflects expanding regional footprint
