# Chapter 8 Review
## Define
1. fish—aquatic vertebrates possessing gills, fins, and scales
2. scale—overlapping bony plates that protrude from a fish's skin
3. myomere—W-shaped bands of skeletal muscle tissue found in fish 
4. gill—organ which a fish uses to extract oxygen from the water
   gill filaments—a long, narrow, fleshy projection that branches off a fish's **gill arch** to absorb oxygen from water
   gill slits—slits through which water that has passed over a fish's gills exits the body
   operculum—hard, movable plate that protects and covers a fish gill
5. lateral line—system of nerve endings that extend over a fish's head and sides, allowing the fish to sense vibration and pressure changes
6. roe—fish eggs
   milt—fluid which male fish emits, containing sperm
   livebearer—fish that bear live young
7. photosphore—light-producing organ found in some fish
8. denticle—any of a shark's placoid scales
   spiracles—in a shark or ray, either of the paired holes behind the eyes that are used for inhalation
9. reptile—cold-blooded vertebrates that have scales and breathe with lungs; class Reptilia 
10. amnion—special protective layer that surrounds a reptile egg
11. chromatosphore—color-containing cell located beneath the skin
12. regenerate—to grow back
13. spectacle—immovable, transparent eye covering of a gecko
dewlap—ornamental throat fan of an iguanid
14. Jacobson's organ—cavity in the roof of a snake's mouth that detects scent molecules on the tongue
15. venom—poisonous liquid that is produced by an animals and contains enzymes and chemicals that destroy portions of a body
fang—long hollow or grooved tooth used by a snake to inject venom into its victim
antivenin—medicine designed to counteract snake venom
pit organ—the special organ that allows viperids to "see" warm objects in total darkness
16. plastron—portion of a turtle's shell that covers its abdomen
carapace—in a turtle, the part of the shell that covers the back
scute—individual "squares" of a turtle's shell
17. tortoise—common term for a turtle that lives only on land and has elephant-like feet
18. amphibian—cold-blooded, four-legged vertebrate that is mostly terrestrial but lives near water and spends an important part of its life cycle in it
19. metamorphosis—changing of an immature hatching, which does not resemble its parents, into an adult, which looks like others of that species
20. estivation—type of dormancy in which the mucous coating on an animal's skin hardens, protecting the animal from drying out in a period of drought

## Explain
1. Cold-blooded animals differ from warm-blooded animals in that they have to warm up their body by using outside sourcing, while warm-blooded animals can use their ways to warm up the body.
2. Ray-finned fish possess fins made of webs of skin supported by rays or spines, while lobe-finned fish have webbed rays attached to the body by a lobe.
3. Four basic types of fish scales are placoid scales, Ganoid scales, ctenoid scale, and cycloid scales. Placoid scales look like teeth and are found on the bodies of sharks and other cartilagionous fish. Ganoid scales are diamond shaped. Cycloid scales are round, and ctenoid scales are also round but the exposed edge has sharp barbs.
4. Because a freshwater fish tends to gain water, while a marine fish tends to lose water, then excretory systems must produce either very dilute or concentrated urine so the fish maintains a proper water balance
5. A fish's swim bladder is a large, gas filled organ located high in a fish's body that allows the fish to stay suspended in any depth of water. When it fills the organ with a lighter or heavier gas, the fish can stay afloat in any depth.
6. Flatfish are known for having eyes on the same side of the head.
7. The primary difference between skates and true rays is that skates lay eggs while true rays give birth to live young. Another difference is that the true rays do not have dorsal fins while skates have two dorsal fins near the tips of their tails.
8. Snakes show God's special design in their eating habits. Snakes were specially designed with a set of double-hinged jaws for consuming the large prey that they catch. Also, during swallowing, the trachea is moved forward so the snake can continue breathing. Its flexible skin stretches to accommodate the large meals that it takes in. 
9. Boas develop eggs inside their bodies and give birth to live young; pythons lay eggs.  Most pythons are Old World snakes and most boas are New World snakes.
10.  Lizards have chromatophores and most have legs. Snakes are legless and slither on the ground. They have a Jacobson's Organ for smelling. Turtles are the only reptiles with shells. Crocodiles are the largest living reptiles with thick-skinned bodies. Tuataras are nocturnal, lizard-like reptiles and have a parietal eye.
11. Three main groups of salamanders are wholly terrestrial, wholly aquatic, and amphibious. Wholly terrestrial salamanders live on land and lay their clutch of twenty to thirty eggs on land. Wholly aquatic salamanders live in the water and lay clutches of up to 500 eggs in the water. They undergo metamorphosis but do not lose their external gills. Amphibious salamanders live on land but return to the water to breed; they are known as newts. They lay clutches of 100 to 400 eggs. Larvae of these species metamorphose into juveniles called efts, which are terrestrial. They may return to the water to breed.
12. The egg hatches, and the tadpoles begin to form legs. It also develops external gills on the sides of its head. When metamorphosis occurs, the hind legs develop before the front legs. As the legs grow longer, the tail shrinks. The digestive system shrinks and the respiratory system grows lungs. 
13. Caecilians are considered as amphibians because they do have vertebrae and do not have scales.
## Apply
1. Fish have a two-chambered heart; birds and mammals have four-chambered hearts. Fish pump the blood to gills to oxygen for exchange; birds and mammals pump the blood to lungs for oxygen exchange. A fish's blood travels directly from the gills to the rest of the body; a bird's or mammal's blood returns from the lungs to the heart to be pumped to the rest of the body.
2. Air breathing lungs of fish are not between lungs and gills; they are either fully gills or fully lungs. They are suited for their habitat and lifestyle.
3. The shark reproductive system is complex compared to that of a bony fish. Sharks do not mature quickly. Sharks do not have swim bladders, instead, their liver is 90% oil, which makes the shark almost neutrally buoyant. Sharks also have gill slits but no operculum. 
4. Hagfish are found in saltwater, l.ampreys are found in freshwater and saltwater. Hagfish do not have a sucking organ, while lampreys do have a sucking organ. Hagfish have slit-like mouths, while lampreys have round mouths. Hagfish have slime glands, while lampreys do not. Both hagfish and lampreys lack jaws, bones, scales, and fins and have pore-like gill openings.
5. Fish scales are bony overlapping plates that protrude form the fish's skin. Reptile scales are folds in their tough skin that have been keratinized.
6. Because caffeine is a stimulant, it will make the body's heart rate and circulatory system operate at a much higher speed, making it circulate the venom throughout the body faster.
