# Section 20.1 Review
1. Because people held superstitious beliefs, such as looking to the stars as gods, they never understood that the stars are controlled by a divine being. They had no foundation as to how the earth was formed and no general principles.
2.  Thomas Aquinas combined Aristotle's teachings with those of the Roman Catholic Church. His ideas were soon well adopted, for the Church later gave them official approval and forbade anyone to oppose them.
3. Aristotle's physics arose from his misunderstanding of who was the true Creator of the universe. Failing to recognize God as the Creator, he met with the same scientific flaws as the Greeks of his time. Only God is eternal, but Aristotle believed that some matter had always existed since the beginning of time. His physics produced nothing to help man subdue the earth in the way that God had commanded.
4. Francis Bacon explained that when the Protestant Reformation came about, there also was a new revealing of scientific evidence. The Church was called upon to account for the misleading and inaccurate doctrines it had produced. He wrote that when the time came for God to orchestrate such a reformation, there would be a renovation of the knowledge that man had lost.
5. Copernicus believed that all the planets, including the earth, revolved around the sun. This theory, known as the heliocentric theory, is now known to be true. However, when Copernicus first released his theory, it was received with much opposition and resistance. In his day, the universe was believed to be geocentric, which meant that the sun and all of the planets revolved around the earth. At first it was largely ignored, censored, heavily criticized, and eventually placed on the Roman Catholic Church's list of prohibited books until 1835. 
6. Kepler discovered the three laws of planetary motion. In the first law, he stated that planets orbit the sun in oval-shaped paths called ellipses. In the second law, he explained that planets closer to the sun move faster. In the third law, he described the relation between the time the planet needs to orbit the sun and its distance away from the sun. 
7. Galileo's observations of the sky through a telescope convinced him that Kepler and Copernicus were correct in their heliocentric views. Galileo developed the law of uniform acceleration, which states that the speed of the body falling in a vacuum accelerates uniformly with time. He also discovered the laws of the pendulum, which came with the precise knowledge to construct the pendulum clock. 
8. Mathematics is the language of science. 
9. The term scientist was coined in 1840. Prior to that time, scientists were called natural philosophers. 
10. Isaac Newton contributed more to science than any other individual. He discovered the famous law of universal gravity, which states that every particle of matter in the univverse attracts every other particle of matter with a force proportional to its quantity of matter, decreasing as the square of the distance decreases.
11. The Puritans took the greatest interest in the work of the scientists than any other group.
12. The first permanent scienfitic socity in the Modern Age was the Royal Socieity, founded in 1662 in London.
13. Albert Einstein is especially remembered for stating the theories of relativity. 
14. Andreas Vesalius is known as the Father of Anatomy. William Harvey described the circulation of the blood in the human body.
