###### Explain
1. Three types of flight feathers are primary flight feathers, secondary flight feathers, and tertiary flight feathers. Primary flight feathers extend from the bird's "hand" to the tips of its wings. The secondary flight feathers extend from the back of the ulna and provide the central section of the wing with the shape necessary for lift. The tertiary flight feathers overlap the secondary flight feathers nearest the bird's body.
2. Birds control their flight using their wings, tails, and even individual feathers.
3. Three types of flights are powered flight, also known as flapping flight, gliding, and intermittent flight. In powered flight, a bird flaps continually to travel forward. When a bird glides, it simply stretches out its wings and coasts "downhill," letting gravity do the work. Sometimes, birds use thermals, or hot air, to gain altitude without expending energy. When a bird glides within a thermal to gain altitude, the bird is soaring. Intermittent flight is where some birds alternate flapping with gliding and coasting to save energy.
4. The chalazae is to hold the yolk body up in place. For proper development the yolk body must remain upright in place. When the egg shifts and rolls the chalazae is designed to be flexible so the yolk body can remain upright.
5. Climbing feet allow birds to climb up or down with equal ease. They usually have two forward toes and two hind toes. Woodpeckers are familiar birds with climbing feet that let them stand on the vertical trunks of trees by grasping the barsks as they hammer for food.
6. Most water birds hatch precocial, meaning that they hatch with down and are able to swim shortly after birth. The oil from their preen glands waterproofs their feathers so they can swim and dive yet surface practically dry and able to fly. 
7. Perching birds are **altricial** (hatching naked and helpless) and most fly well, while game birds fly very little and are mostly **precocial** (hatching with down and able to run or swim shortly after birth).
8. Flightless birds
#### APPLY
1. Because birds need to fly, they must not be too heavy else they will not take off. God designed birds to have lightweight hollow bones supported by struts. God also designed their muscles to meet the power demands of flight. The pectorals flap the wings of the bird to lift the bird off the ground and reproduce the speed needed for flying. The sternum has a keel. 
2. The differences between a bird's respiratory system and that of a mammal are needed so that the bird can fly in high altitudes. All mammals have a diaphragm. In contrast, birds do not have a diaphragm. In place of a diaphragm, air is pumped through the lungs as chest muscles expand and contract the thoracic cavity. A bird's lungs do not have alveoli, while a mammal's lungs do have alveoli. Birds have tiny tubes in their lungs called air capillaries, instead, that allow air to flow through the lungs instead of only flowing in and out like mammals. This allows for high oxygen transfer rates at great altitudes. Birds inhale and exhale twice to move each breath of air through its respiratory system; mammals only inhale and exhale once to move each breath through its respiratory system.
