# Section Review 7.1
1. The bones of birds are hollow and connected by struts to be much more lightweight than the bones of other animals. 
2. The keel is a large ridge which allows the flight muscles with a powerful attachment point.
3. The most important flight muscles in a bird are the **pectorals**.
4. *crop—* A storage sac in the esophagus of birds which moistens food and releases it in a steady stream to the rest of the digestive system.
5. The **gizzard** makes up for the bird's lack of teeth. This special section in its stomach grinds food to make it easily digestible. 
6. The contour feathers provide a bird's streamlined shape.
7. *shaft, barbs (barbules), and a vane*
8. Birds preen to keep their feathers in good condition for flying.
9. *molting—* The process of birds shedding their feathers to replace lost, broken, or old ones.
###### Thought Provokers
1. Because a bird inhales and exhales twice to move each breath of air through its respiratory system, it essentially takes two breaths at once. This allows for birds to extract the maximum amount of oxygen from every breath they take.
2. Monocular vision provides songbirds with a wide field of vision for detecting danger. This makes it very hard for predators to sneak up on them.
