# Section 17.3

1. Colonial government in the days of the past were a reflection of her mother country, Britain; however, they were uniquely American. They established self-government and and had written charters which functioned as early constitutions. Legislatures convened in every state except Pennsylvania. Each state also had a governor who served as its main overseer.

2. England and Scotland merged in **1707** to form the nation of **Great Britain.**

3. **George III**.

4. The **French and Indian War** prepared the colonists for war against the British and united them against a common foe. The **Peace of Paris** ended the French and Indian War.

5. The colonists hated the **Stamp Act of 1765** the most.

6. The **Townshend Acts** forced the colonists to pay tax on several types of new imports into the colonies. It established a board of customs in Boston to oversee customs collection and created special courts to try customs dodgers without a jury. This imposition caused the colonists to react in fury and insist that this was unconstitutional under taxation without representation. A boycott began when the colonists again refused to purchase British goods.

7. The **Committees of Correspondence** were formed to keep Americans informed of the conflict with England.

8. At the Boston Tea Party, several colonists dressed up as Native Americans boarded ships docked in the Boston Harbor and, using tomahawks, opened up and threw into the harbor roughly 340 chests of tea. The British, enraged at this act, enacted a series of measures called the "Intolerable Acts" by the Americans. This included the closing of the Boston Harbor to all commerce, the placement of soldiers in every colony to preserve order, and other laws which restricted self-governance by the colonists and infringed traditional English liberties.

9. The first shots of the **War for Independence** were fired on the morning of **April 19, 1775**, in **Lexington, Massachusetts**.

10. Gorge Washington was chosen to lead the **Continental Army**.

11. The **Declaration of Independence** is the most important human statement of political principles ever written in the history of the world. It was accepted unanimously on **July 4, 1776.**

### Terms

1. *Mayflower—* The ship that carried the Pilgrims to the United States

2. Mayflower Compact—Document signed by the Pilgrims to instigate a form of self-governance

3. Methodist—Denomination started by John Wesley; named for the regularity of their meetings and their well-ordered methods of conduct.

4. Half-Way Covenant—A rule passed by the Puritans which allowed unconverted relatives of church members to join the churches.

5. mercantilism—an early European economic theory which measured a nation's wealth by the amount of gold and silver it possessed.

6. written charters—documents which functioned as comprehensive written constitutions

7. legislatures—elected representatives which convened in each state to decide state issues

8. governor—one who served in each colony as its chief executive officer

9. Townshend Acts—Acts imposed on the colonies by Great Britain, named for King George's Chancellor of the Exchequer, Charles Townshend which forced colonists to pay duties on various types of imports into the colonies

10. Intolerable Acts—Acts imposed on the colonies by Great Britain, enacted to punish the colonists for the **Boston Tea Party**.

11. Declaration of Independence—The most important human statement of political principles in the history of the world

12. Articles of Confederation—The first basis for a national government of the United States

13. United States Constitution—A document which serves as a basis for the governance of the United States.

14. Monroe Doctrine—A proclamation issued by U.S. President James Monroe which warned that Western Hemisphere nations were off-limits for European nations to colonize.

15. 13th Amendment—The amendment which outlawed slavery in the United States.

### Events

1. Pietist Movement—A movement which sparked increased Bible study movement in Germany and Scandinavia.

2. Great Awakening—A revival in America

3. Boston Tea Party—An event in which several hundred colonists dressed up as native Americans and boarded several tea ships sitting in the Boston Harbor and dumped 340 chests of tea into the harbor.

5. Treaty of Paris—The treaty that settled the War for Independence.

9. Second Great Awakening—The second nationwide revival in the United States

### Concepts to Consider

1. The people on the Mayflower set the example for self-governance and signed the Mayflower compact, which was a basis for the later US Constitution. It also represented the nation America would later become. It fostered a spirit of toleration, which allows other to practice religious beliefs regardless of your own views or opinions. It was not just out of necessity, but out of principle, because beliefs are a matter of conscience and not to be forced.

2. The French and Indian War prepared colonial America for the revolution against Great Britain. It united them against a common enemy and gave them valuable military experience.

3. The preaching that the colonists had heard during the Great Awakening helped prepare them to recognize that all men are equal in the sight of God. It also importantly drew them together into one nation under God.
