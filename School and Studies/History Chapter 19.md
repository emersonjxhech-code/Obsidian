# Section 19.1 Review
1. Unlike much of Europe, which was engaged in violent revolution, England was able to work reforms through the collective will of its citizens and the cooperation of Parliament. England was noted for her progress towards more *representative government, the establishment of religious toleration, and the growth of the British Empire*, as well as for the reign of **Queen Victoria**, the *longest ruling monarch in English history.*
2. **Queen Victoria** was the longest ruling monarch in English history. When she learned that she was likely to become the next queen, she reasoned, *"I will be good."*
3. Her husband was **Prince Albert** of *Germany*, the *prince consort*.  **Benjamin Disraeli** had the most influence upon her of any prime minister.
4. The secret of **Victorian England's** success was the Bible.
5. In 1850, the liberals represented the middle class of the Industrial Age. The Conservatives represented the nobility and other wealthy people. The leader of the Liberals was **William Gladstone**, while the leader of the Conservatives was **Benjamin Disraeli**.
6. Religion has always prevented peace between *England and Ireland*. England has always remained predominantly Protestant since the days of Henry VIII, while Ireland has remained predominantly Catholic. Much of the bloodshed of the centuries has been a result of this issue.
7. In the 20th century, Ireland was divided into two portions—the **Irish Free State**, with membership in the Commonwealth of Nations, and the six counties of **northeast Ulster** remained a part of the United Kingdom. Today, the Irish Free State is completely independent of the Commonwealth of Nations, and is known as just **Ireland**, and the six counties of Ulster are known as **Northern Ireland**. 
8. Three words that sum up the reason of British expansion are **gold, God, and glory**.  The economic factors were important from the very beginning. During the golden age of British capitalism, businessmen wanted raw materials to fuel their industries. They also wanted the open markets and the investment opportunities which the empire afforded them. The religious factors grew as the sincere Christian convictions of many Englishmen led them to promote missions in such a way that the 19th century became the **Great Century of Missions**. The patriotic factors included pride for their queen, their flag, their fleet, and their empire. The British people eagerly followed the growth of the empire. They were fascinated by the stories, goods, fantastic animals that were brought back by explorers.

# Section 19.4 Review
1. The largest continent in the world is **Asia**. Africa follows as the second largest continent.

## Concepts to Consider
1. Great Britian benefited from her colonies because of imports. Imports such as cotton, sugar, tea, rubber, precious metals, and other products which fueled British economy. The colonies also served as markets for British manufactured goods.  It also allowed Britain to gain global power and shape international affairs, control vital trade routes, and establish itself as a superpower. The colonies benefited from British resources, particularly transportation systems which were built in most British colonies, such as railways, telegraph lines, and ports. They benefited from a degree of order such as the legal system and predictability, as well as education. They had access to British technology, markets, and capital.
