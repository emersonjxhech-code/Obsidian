## People
1. Julius Caesar—Conquered the Celts about fifty years before the birth of Christ. 
2. Augustine—Missionary appointed by Pope Gregory the Great in 597 who converted the Anglo-Saxons to Roman Catholicism. 
3. Alfred the Great—First great king of England; helped to make England a nation-state, began the *Anglo-Saxon Chronicle*.
4. Canute the Dane—Became king of England and ruled England, Denmark, and Norway as a great empire.
5. William, duke of Normandy—Defeated Harold Godwinson at the Norman Conquest (Battle of Hastings), became king of England; abolished the Witan and began the Great Council, kept records in the *Domesday Book.*
6. Henry II—Descendant of William the Conqueror, became king of England and began the Plantagenet line of kings, strengthened the national army and improved the royal courts; developed the common laws and instituted trial by jury.
7. Richard I—Known as Richard the Lion-Hearted and for his love of Crusades to the Holy Land.
8. King John—The most hated king of English history.
9. Simon de Montfort—Led rebellion against Henry III, took control of the government and called what is remembered as the first meeting of Parliament in 1265. 
10. Edward I—King of England who longed to unite the whole island of Great Britain into one kingdom. He subdued Wales after five years but was less successful with Scotland.
11. Joan of Arc—French peasant girl who claimed to hear "heavenly voices" and led the French army into battle. Won a few temporary victories but was soon captured and executed by the English.
12. Henry IV—King of England, began the Lancastrian dynasty.
13. Henry VII—First Tudor king of England. During his reign, the exploration of the world began.
14. Henry VIII—King of England, Defender of the Roman faith, held absolute religious and political power. 
15. Edward VI—Protestant king of England.
16. Mary I—First daughter of Henry VIII, known as Bloody Mary, slaughtered Protestant martyrs in an attempt to restore England to Roman Catholicism.
17. John Foxe—Author of *Foxe's Book of Martyrs*.
18. Elizabeth I—Queen of England, known as Good Queen Bess, defeated the *Spanish Armada*.
19. Philip II—King of Spain who attempted to invade the Netherlands and England but failed.
20. Mary Stuart—Queen of the Scots, cousin of Elizabeth, was executed by order of Elizabeth.
21. John Knox—Outstanding leader of the Scottish Reformation.
22. William the Silent, Prince of Orange—Leader of the Dutch patriots, was assassinated by a Spanish agent in 1584. 
23. John Hawkins—Englishman who was hired by Queen Elizabeth to build up the English fleet.
24. Sir Francis Drake—Attacker and raider of Spanish ships.
25. William Shakespeare—The greatest writer the world has ever known.
26. James I—The first Stuart monarch of England, had the King James Authorized Bible after him.
27. Charles I—King of England, hated Parliament, began the "Eleven Years Tyranny".
28. Oliver Cromwell—Leader of the New Model Army (Ironsides) in the English Civil War, began the Commonwealth of England. 
29. Charles II—King of England who restored the monarchy, obtained toleration for English Catholics and joined the Roman Church in the Treaty of Dover in exchange for money from French King Louis XIV for foreign policy.
30. James II—Catholic king of England who tried unsuccessfully to reimpose Romanism on England.
31. William III and Mary II—Dutch joint rulers of England who were offered the throne, established the English Bill of Rights. During their reign, the Glorious Revolution of 1688 occurred.
## Terms
1. Celts—Earliest known inhabitants of the British Isles. 
   Druids—A special group of Celtic men who acted as teachers, priests, and judges.
2. Anglo-Saxons—The collective name of the Jutes, Angles, and Saxons.
3. *Beowulf*—Greatest Saxon poem that remains.
4. Norman Conquest—Battle of Hastings where William the Conqueror defeated Harold Godwinson in 1066. 
5. *Domesday Book*—A book of census results for King William the duke of Normandy.
6. Plantagenet—Line of kings so named for the sprig of broom *plante genêt* that Henry II's father Geoffrey, count of Anjou wore in his hat.
7. Magna Carta—Great Charter; one of the most important documents in history; clearly defined the ancient rights and privileges of the people.
8. Constitution—Rules for exercise and restraint of governmental power.
9. Model Parliament—First representative government model after which Parliament became a regular feature of English governance, established by Edward I.
10. *Book of Common Prayer*—A book compiled by Thomas Cranmer which provided prayers to be spoken or sung.
11. Puritans—Dissatisfied Protestants that wanted to purify the Church of England to make it less like the Roman Church.
12. King James or Authorized Version—Best loved and most used translation of God's Word ever produced; published in 1611.
13. Petition of Right—A document drawn up by Parliament which merely reaffirmed the liberties and rights of Englishmen as started in the Magna Carta.
14. Roundheads—A side in the English Civil War against the King, characterized by their short hair in the shape of a bowl.
Cavaliers—Those for the King, characterized by wearing their hair in long ringlets after the French fashion.
15. Ironsides—Those among the army of Oliver Cromwell (New Model Army).
16. Commonwealth—England's name for the new government, headed by Oliver Cromwell, which claimed to be a republic.
17. Protectorate—New government set up by Cromwell.
18. Whigs—A political faction in Parliament which arose to oppose Charles's Catholic sympathies and his efforts to secure Catholic succession to the throne.

## Events
1. Hundred Years' War—A war fought between England and France which was primarily triggered by a dispute over the French throne.
2. War of the Roses—A war between the York and Lancaster families; so named because the Yorks used a white rose as their emblem and the Lancasters used a red rose as theirs.
3. Battle of Bosworth Field—Final battle of the War of the Roses in which Henry Tudor (Lancaster) defeated Richard III (York) and became Henry VII, the first Tudor king of England.
4. English Reformation—A reformation that resulted in the Church of England breaking away from the authority of the Pope and the Roman Catholic Church.
5. Scottish Reformation—A reformation that led Scotland to break away from Roman Catholic Church and establish the Scottish Presbyterian Church.
6. Eleven Years' Tyranny—A tyranny by Charles I which was caused by the dissolving of Parliament. During this time, the government of England was changed from a government by monarchs, lord, and commons, to what was in effect an absolute and irrepressible monarchy, like that of France or Spain.
7. English Civil War—The English Civil War (1642–1651) was a series of conflicts between Royalists loyal to King Charles I and Parliamentarians led by Oliver Cromwell, resulting in the king's execution, the temporary abolition of the monarchy, and the establishment of a Commonwealth. 
8. Treaty of Dover—A treaty signed by Charles II with King Louis XIV of France in which Charles would receive money for his foreign policy, and would have to obtain toleration of English Catholics and join the Roman church himself.
9. Glorious Revolution—Also known as the Bloodless Revolution, the Glorious Revolution of 1688 established the principles of consent by the governed for once and for all. It set the standard by which future English kings and queens would be chosen.

## Dates to Remember
1. 1066—Battle of Hastings, Norman Conquest.
2. 1215—Magna Carta signed.
3. 1588—Spanish Armada defeated.
4. 1611—King James or Authorized Version of the Bible completed.
5. 1662—English monarchy fully restored.
6. 1688—Glorious or Bloodless Revolution of 1688.

## Concepts to Consider
1. The Magna Carta set the precedents for taxation without representation, habeas corpus, trial by jury, and due process of law. It set a limit on royal power, meaning that the king was governed by the law itself. The Model Parliament set the example for representative government. 
2. England's defeat of the Spanish Armada confirmed England's freedom from Spanish rule forever. It also ensured that Protestantism could thrive and that Catholicism would not rule the world. It represented the weakening of Spain's power and that it would not rule the world forever.
