###### Thought Provoker
Horns have a bony core surrounded by a hollow sheath of hardened skin called a horn. Horns are usually found on both males and females, while antlers are typically found only on adult male deer, though some female deer, such as reindeer, can also have antlers. When fully grown, antlers are dead bone without a covering. Horns are permanent in almost all bovids and continue to grow throughout life. They are used as tools for protection, while antlers are used as weapons against rival bucks and to protect does.

###### Thought Provoker
1. The platypus is often considered undeveloped by evolutionists because of its differences as a mammal. However, its unique bill demonstrates the contrary. As a quick hunter, the platypus can stay submerged for only one to two minutes. When the bill is placed in water, thousands of tiny holes in the platypus's beak open. Muscular contractions created by the platypus's prey generate electricity, which triggers about 40,000 receptors on the platypus's beak. These receptors are arranged in such a way as to allow the platypus to determine the precise location of its prey. Another 60,000 receptors are used to respond to touch. These receptors detect water currents stimulated by the prey, which enables the platypus to determine the distance to its prey. Working together, the electrical and touch receptors allow the platypus to use its bill to "see" underwater. This very advanced technique of fishing shows God's magnificent creative power.

# Chapter 6 Review

###### Define
1. Vertebrate—an animal with a backbone.
   Invertebrate—an animal without a backbone.
2. Warm-blooded—animals that regulate their body temperature internally.
   Hibernation—the process by which mammals become dormant in the winter, involving a reduction in body temperature close to the freezing point of water.
3. Endoskeleton—an internal skeleton found in mammals.
4. Mammary glands—glands in female mammals that allow them to nourish their young.
5. Camouflage—the act of blending into the environment to hide from predators or sneak up on prey.
   Vibrissae—specialized hair structures on an animal's whiskers, surrounded by sensitive nerve endings.
6. Carnivore—an animal that eats mainly meat.
   Herbivore—an animal that primarily eats plants.
   Omnivore—an animal that eats both meat and plants.
7. Amniotic sac—a sac in female mammals that contains amniotic fluid to surround and protect the developing young by absorbing shock.
8. Prehensile tail—a tail that is capable of grabbing objects.
9. Monkey—a primate with a tail.
   Ape—a primate without a tail.
10. Baleen whale—a whale with comb-like plates in its mouth used for filtering food from the water.
    Toothed whale—a whale that has sharp teeth for catching and eating prey.
11. Bovid—a group of artiodactyls that includes cattle, sheep, goats, and antelopes.
12. Horn—a bony core surrounded by a hollow sheath of hardened skin.
    Antler—dead bone that grows on adult male deer.
13. Oviparous—animals that reproduce by laying eggs.
14. Extinct—a species that was once alive but has been wiped out by hunting, natural occurrences, or other causes and is now nonexistent.
    Endangered—any animal species with a low population that is in danger of becoming extinct.

###### Explain
1. Movement is likely the most notable characteristic that distinguishes animals from other living things.
2. Xenarthrans are known for additional joints in their lower back vertebrae.
3. Binocular vision gives primates excellent depth perception.
4. New World monkeys live in tropical forests in Central and South America, while Old World monkeys are found in Africa and Asia. Most New World monkeys have prehensile tails, whereas Old World monkeys do not. New World monkeys have widely spaced, round nostrils and thick, woolly fur, while Old World monkeys have closely spaced, comma-shaped nostrils.
5. Dolphins have sharp, cone-shaped teeth, while porpoises have flattened, spade-shaped teeth. The dolphin's dorsal fin curves, whereas the porpoise's fin is triangular. The dolphin has a pointed beak, while the porpoise's head and beak are rounded. The dolphin's jaw is triangular, while the porpoise's is rounded. Dolphins are typically eight to twelve feet long, while porpoises are usually about six feet long. Dolphins are particularly social and outgoing, whereas porpoises are shy and wary of humans.
6. Lagomorphs differ from rodents in that they have four upper incisor teeth.
7. All perissodactyls have an odd number of weight-bearing toes on each foot.
8. Bovids are likely the most useful artiodactyls because they provide mankind with food and clothing.
9. The aardvark is a xenarthran.
10. Opossums are the only marsupials found outside Australia and its surrounding islands.
11. Placental mammals nourish their young inside the uterus via the placenta. Marsupials give birth to highly underdeveloped young, which live in their pouches until fully developed. Monotremes, or egg-laying mammals, hatch from eggs and are nourished by their mother's milk.
12. Hunting a particular animal species repeatedly and excessively or destroying its habitat can cause the extinction of that species. The Flood likely caused most of the extinctions that have occurred.
