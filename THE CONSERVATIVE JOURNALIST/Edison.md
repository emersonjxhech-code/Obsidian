## Where to Cast

- The Outlet (Easier Access): The most accessible fishing is near the outlet side where the water is shallower and easy to wade or cast from the bank. This area is best for using dry flies like the Mosquito.
- Boulders & Log Jams: For larger trout, venture toward the boulder fields at the base of the Norwegian Buttress. Here, the water drops off quickly to deeper ledges where trout often hide among downed trees.
- Lunch Rock: This massive rock along the shore is a popular casting platform. While it offers great distance, be mindful that trout may be holding directly against the rock's base in the rain.

## Rainy Day Techniques

- Stealth Advantage: Use the rain to your advantage. The "pock-marked" surface from raindrops breaks up your silhouette, allowing you to approach closer to the bank without spooking fish.
- Fan Casting: Since trout cruise the shoreline in the rain, use a fan-shaped casting pattern. Start with short casts parallel to the bank, then progressively cast further out to cover all depths.
- Slow Retrieval: In the cooler water tomorrow (51°F), trout may be slightly less explosive than on a warm day. If using an Olive Woolly Bugger, use a slow, pulsing strip along the rocky drop-offs.
- Targeting "Seams": Look for areas where the rain-driven surface current meets the still water of the basin. These "seams" act as conveyor belts for food.
